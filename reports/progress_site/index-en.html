Why Retrain has to come before PGA/FedEraser

Any claim of the form "method X makes the model forget client A" only means something when compared against Mretrain(−A) — a model trained from scratch that never saw A's data. This is the gold standard: without it there is no basis to say whether PGA or FedEraser actually works. That is why Week 13–14 focused on building the Retrain pipeline and a way to measure "how close to retrain" before touching any real unlearning algorithm.

Both models are trained from the same starting point (w0) and the same seed; the only difference is whether the target client A takes part in training. Hover (or tap) each rule button to see how A is chosen.

T1 · median size

Sorts clients by (sample count, ID), picks the client at the median position. With 10 clients (an even number), the current rule takes the lower median — a convention not specified in the research plan, pending confirmation.

Why choose before training?

Rules T1/T2/T3 are applied to the data partition, never to the results — avoiding a repeat of an earlier issue when choosing the μ hyperparameter (picking after seeing good results biases the conclusion). Both canonical configs currently leave target_selection_rule: null on purpose; the runner refuses to start as-is — this is one of the points pending confirmation.

Even two retrains that are otherwise identical (both excluding client A) end up as slightly different models, purely because of a different random seed. That difference is the noise floor — the threshold for calling a model "like retrain" or not.

The thesis's own convention — no precedent found in the literature The noise-floor method (comparing every pair among several independent retrains to build a discrimination threshold) is this thesis's own proposal. The full literature_matrix.md and gap table were checked again: no FU paper in the catalog uses this exact approach to set a "looks like retrain" threshold. This is a point that needs confirmation before it becomes the thesis's main criterion.

The formula, in words

Take 5 retrains excluding A with different seeds (the set 42 plus the 2 extra seeds already used by the main grid, times 2 ways of varying the seed). Compare the accuracy distance between every pair of retrains — that is the "natural spread" even though nothing is wrong. Threshold = mean + 2×standard deviation of those distances. A model Mx counts as "inside the noise floor" (= looks like retrain, may have actually forgotten A) if its distance from the retrains does not exceed this threshold.

The thesis runs two floor variants at once: Floor A only varies minibatch order (keeps the same initial weights); Floor B also varies the initial weights. Both are reported side by side, never merged into a single number.

Closest quantitative neighbor to watch: GDFA

An earlier scan (not part of November) verified that GDFA (CVPR 2026) directly benchmarks PGA and SIFU — two methods this thesis also uses — on the same dataset family (MNIST/FMNIST/CIFAR-10/CIFAR-100), the same Dirichlet-α convention, and outperforms both at α = 0.5. However GDFA only tests one α level in its main results table, without sweeping the full IID→0.1 range — exactly the axis this thesis's proposed direction (conditioning intensity on the target's Non-IID severity) aims to exploit.

Other papers checked earlier — Lethe (a benchmark, not a competing method; its finding reinforces exactly why this thesis needs noise-floor/MIA evidence) and FedCCCU (unlearns at the class-within-client level, not a whole client, with no Non-IID-severity parameter) — do not overlap directly either.
